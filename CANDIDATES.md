# Candidates — Charm the Commuter: Forecast a City That Isn't

DeepSeek (via 100milionseek) was down for the whole brainstorm: 5 calls,
all HTTP 503 Service Unavailable (3 parallel generation calls, then 2
retries with 20s and 60s waits). Per the brief, candidates were generated
and critique-picked by the builder instead, so the build was not blocked.

Judge criteria (in order): (1) reads as a one-line forecast for a city
that does not exist, city named, weather the whole point; (2) makes a
bored commuter smile on one reading; (3) keeps real forecast register /
broadcast rhythm; (4) one concrete detail, so it feels like a place;
(5) uses its 140 characters well. Quirkiness for its own sake does not
count.

## Candidates (all ≤ 140 chars)

- A (127): "Today in Puddlejump: scattered showers, a high of 14°, and every puddle is exactly ankle-deep — choose your battles, commuters."
- B (136): "Good morning, Drizzlewick: drizzle until noon, then drizzle with ambition. Puddles have unionised; bring a brolly and a sense of humour."
- C (134): "In Bumbershoot Bay: rain easing by evening, with a 90% chance your bus arrives the moment the rain stops. Timing, as ever, impeccable."
- D (121): "Today in Monsoon Mews: heavy rain all day, but the ducks have started a ferry service — your commute just got an upgrade."
- E (121): "Cloudburst-on-Sea: sunshine at nine, hail at ten, rainbow at eleven — the council apologises for the scheduling conflict."
- F (116): "Today in Fogborough: fog thick enough to lose your bus in, clearing by noon. Your umbrella finally gets the day off."
- G (117): "In Gusty Gulch: 50 mph winds, hold on to your hat and your sandwich — the seagulls have learned to surf the updrafts."
- H (124): "In Drizzleford: rain, rain, and — plot twist — more rain, but the bus shelter has free Wi-Fi and a roof that actually works."

## Critique

- A: strongest forecast register ("Today in X: scattered showers, a
  high of 14°"), city named, weather the point, one vivid concrete
  detail (ankle-deep puddles), and the joke speaks to the commuter
  directly — "choose your battles" is the smile on one reading. Every
  word earns its place.
- B: "drizzle with ambition" is the best single turn of phrase here,
  but two jokes crowd the line and "a sense of humour" leans preachy.
- C: dry and very commuter-relatable, but the joke is about the bus,
  not the weather — weaker on criterion 1.
- D: runner-up. The ducks' ferry service is the most surprising image
  and the weather stays the point, but it addresses the commuter less
  directly than A.
- E: good rhythm and a dry council joke, but the joke is about the
  council, not the commuter's morning.
- F: "lose your bus in" the fog is a great image and the umbrella's
  day off is charming; uses fewer of its 140 characters than A.
- G: fun, but the seagull image is quirk for its own sake; the
  sandwich line carries it.
- H: "plot twist" is a nice turn and the shelter is concrete, but
  "rain, rain, more rain" is the weakest forecast register of the set.

## Pick

**A — the Puddlejump line.** It is the only candidate that is top-tier
on all five criteria at once: forecast rhythm intact, invented city
named, weather the whole point, one concrete detail, and a joke aimed
squarely at the bored commuter reading it at the bus stop.
